Democrats flip the Georgia House during the national election wave

The 2026 election changed power not only in Congress but also in state government. In Georgia, Democrats won enough seats to take control of the state House after entering the campaign as the minority. The party also captured the governorship, giving it control of those institutions for the first time in more than two decades.

The result was especially notable because Republicans had held a comfortable advantage in the lower chamber before voting began. Competitive suburban districts, changing demographics, and the national political climate all contributed to the shift. Final control of some other closely divided state chambers remained unsettled while election officials completed counts.

Across the country, Democratic gains in state legislatures gave the party new authority over budgets, election rules, education, and health policy. The state results also reinforced the conclusion drawn from the congressional contests: voter dissatisfaction extended beyond a small set of federal battlegrounds and affected races at several levels of government.
