Democrats capture Congress after a broad midterm victory

Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the 2026 United States midterm elections, ending unified Republican control in Washington. The House result was driven by gains in competitive suburban districts and by several victories in places that had recently favored Republicans. Senate contests also moved toward the Democrats, including races in states that had not elected a Democratic senator for many years.

Voters repeatedly identified the cost of living and concern about government conduct as important issues. President Donald Trump had made the contest a test of his administration, so the result was widely read as a rejection of the governing party. Democratic leaders said their first priorities would be affordability and oversight when the new Congress convenes in January.

Republican leaders began discussing how the party should respond after losing its majorities. The scale of the defeat also opened leadership contests in Congress and a debate about campaign strategy, candidate selection, redistricting, and the administration's agenda.
