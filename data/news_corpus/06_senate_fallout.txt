Senate Republicans confront losses and questions about direction

Republican senators returned to Washington facing a leadership decision and an internal review after Democrats won a series of unexpected races. The opposition gained seats in states including Texas, Kansas, South Carolina, Ohio, Iowa, North Carolina, and Maine, according to election reports. The results gave Democrats control of the Senate in the next Congress.

Republican lawmakers disagreed about how much of the defeat should be attributed to the White House, campaign tactics, or the party's message on living costs. Some called for major changes and argued that an election of this scale could not be followed by business as usual. Others defended the administration's policy record while accepting that the party had failed to persuade enough voters.

The leadership debate will affect the remaining lame-duck session as well as the Republican strategy in opposition. Before January, the current majority is expected to focus on pending nominations and unfinished legislation. Democrats, meanwhile, are preparing to use their new majority and the momentum of a national victory.
