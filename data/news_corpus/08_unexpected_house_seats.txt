Democrats win House districts that had appeared out of reach

The size of the Democratic House victory became clearer as results arrived from districts that party strategists had not treated as top targets. Candidates defeated Republican incumbents in parts of Wisconsin, Texas, New Jersey, and other states, expanding the map beyond the best-known battlegrounds. Some of the losing Republicans had previously been viewed as secure or as potential candidates for higher office.

These results mattered for more than the final size of the majority. They suggested that the national environment had moved far enough to overcome local advantages, established fundraising networks, and recent Republican voting patterns. Democratic donors and campaign committees had invested less heavily in several of the races than they had in conventional swing seats.

Republicans also failed to gain many of the Democratic districts they had hoped to capture. Combined with losses in their own territory, that failure produced a much smaller conference and transferred the House to Democratic control. Both parties will study the surprise districts when deciding where to recruit candidates and spend money in the next election.
